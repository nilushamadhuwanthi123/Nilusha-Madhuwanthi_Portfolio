01 — About

Engineering discipline, practiced in public.

I'm an undergraduate at SLIIT who treats every personal repo as a place to practice real engineering discipline — not just make something that works once. Most of my featured projects are deliberately framework-free: before I let React or Next.js abstract something away, I build it in plain JavaScript first, so I understand exactly what I'm trading for convenience.

Right now that means juggling a full course load with two internships at once — a Software Engineering role at EgoTechWorld and a Frontend Development role at CodeAlpha — plus three completed levels of a development internship at Codveda. Seven of my personal projects are live and deployed, not just source sitting in a repo.

Security and correctness aren't an afterthought: bcrypt hashing, CSRF tokens, prepared statements and row-locked database transactions show up throughout my work, including in a full banking-system rebuild with ACID-safe money movement.

  • No eval()Every expression, calculator and parser I write is routed through a real parsing library or my own logic — never a raw eval.
  • No framework I don't understandVanilla JS first, framework second — so the abstraction is a choice, not a crutch.
  • No shortcuts on a11yKeyboard navigation and screen-reader support are part of the build, not a pass at the end.
  • Ship it liveA project isn't done until it's deployed somewhere a stranger can actually open it.
